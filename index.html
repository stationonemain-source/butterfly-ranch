501(c)(3) horse rescue · Norman, Oklahoma

Every horse here gets followed, from the day it arrives.

We take in horses from abusive homes, kill pens and surrenders. Once a horse is rehabilitated, it is introduced to students in our program. Each summer a student is assigned a horse to care for, and some go on to become its owner.

How a horse comes through

Rescue, rehab, and a student of its own

  1. 1

    Rescue

    Horses come to us from dangerous places: kill pens, abusive homes, and owners who can no longer keep them.

  2. 2

    Rehabilitation

    Feed, supplements, farrier, vet care and time. The work goes at the horse's pace, not ours.

  3. 3

    A student of its own

    When a horse is ready, it meets the students in our program. Each summer a student is assigned a horse to care for, and some go on to become its owner.

About

The people at the barn

Braxton started Butterfly Ranch in 2022 because the community needed a place where kids and adults could spend time with horses and get away from city life.

Braxton Brackin

Founder & CEO

Raised around rodeo and riding for 27 years, Braxton learned calf roping from her uncle before she could walk. She still competes, and teaches with a firm but encouraging style that helps students believe they can do anything they set their minds to.

Anna Foote

Barn Manager & Instructor

Born and raised in Cody, Wyoming, Anna rode six years with a rodeo drill team before moving to Oklahoma after college in 2023. She loves breakaway roping and teaching riders of every age, and making it fun.

Chloe Bjornberg

Instructor

A former president of the OU Equestrian Team, Chloe has taught English and western lessons for over eight years. She's now in graduate school at OU, working toward becoming a counselor who offers equine-assisted therapy.
