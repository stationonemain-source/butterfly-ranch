01Project overview

Butterfly Rescue Ranch saves horses, but people cannot see it happen. The class's brief names the problem as a lack of consistency across channels, and its insight is that donors, volunteers and neighbours would get involved if they could see what their help does.

The ranch's current site, butterflyrescueranch.org, is a three-page Wix site (Home, About, Donate). Its Donate page has no way to pay and asks people to contact Braxton; there is no volunteer form, no horse pages and no costs. Read against the brief, the gaps are:

GapWhat the brief says
No single homeThe Wix site carries the name and logo but nothing to do: no way to give, no volunteer form, no horse to follow. Name and message still differ between the site and the social bios.
Giving is unclearThe donation method is undecided (Venmo, GoFundMe, check or cash).
Thank-yous depend on memoryDonors and volunteers stop when their help goes unnoticed; there is no set process to thank or update them.
Impact is invisibleThere is no itemized monthly cost per horse, so no dollar can be shown going anywhere.
Followers are not a listSocial followers are not captured into an email list the ranch owns.

The strategy. Every horse gets a page and a story, from arrival to recovery. Every gift and every volunteer hour is thanked within a day and tied back to a horse. Station builds the systems once; the class, and then the OU intern, feed them stories.

A. One home: the custom website

  • One site with the ranch's name, logo and one mission statement on every page, so the brief's mandatories hold everywhere.
  • Our Horses: a page for each horse the ranch names, following it through Arrival, Rehabilitation, Meets a student and Assigned, with intake photos as the before. The ranch does not rehome: a recovered horse is assigned to a student in the summer program, who may go on to own it.
  • Where Your Gift Goes: the itemized monthly cost of caring for one horse, from the cost sheet the ranch provides.
  • Donate and Volunteer buttons on every page, leading to the donation pages (B) and a volunteer form that feeds Pursuit (C).
  • A links page for every social bio, replacing Linktree, with the same two buttons at the top.
  • Email sign-up on every page.

A working concept, built on the ranch's own photos, logo and colors, is at stationonemain-source.github.io/butterfly-ranch (unlisted). It shows the horse journal, the monthly cost table, the volunteer form and the program price list with the ranch's 2025 camp and lesson prices. Blanks in it are the items in Section 09.

B. Make giving easy: Tap (Station Give)

  • One-time and monthly gifts by card, Apple Pay, Google Pay or bank, on the ranch's own Stripe account, from the website, every bio and every flyer. Money goes straight to the ranch; Station takes none of it.
  • Sponsor a horse: Friend, Sponsor and Business levels, billed monthly, with the sponsor's first name on the horse's page if they opt in.
  • A tax receipt for every gift, sent immediately. Every monthly receipt carries a link to update the card or stop the gift; a failed card gets an update-your-card email and is retried automatically.
  • Invoices for business sponsors and partners who give by business card or want a receipt for a set amount.
  • Venmo, GoFundMe and checks can stay as secondary options; the card checkout is the one the campaign promotes.

C. Thank, update and keep: Dispatch, Pursuit, Revive

  • Every one-time gift, and the first payment of every monthly gift, sends a personal thank-you in Braxton's voice about 20 minutes later, and adds the donor to the list.
  • Every volunteer sign-up gets a same-day welcome by text and email and a link to book orientation, followed up until they reply (up to 5 texts and 5 emails over 14 days), drawing on the plan's shared 1,900 texts and 25,000 emails a month.
  • One pipeline for every open volunteer, sponsor, partner and adopter enquiry.
  • A monthly email to the list, built from templates in the editor: which horses arrived, which moved a stage, and what last month's gifts paid for, from the same shared 25,000 emails a month.
  • Win-back waves to people who gave or volunteered once and went quiet: up to 5,000 contacts a wave, a wave every quarter.

D. Answer everyone: Greet, Slate, Lineback, Frontdesk

  • Website chat on every page that answers how to donate, volunteer and visit, and captures the visitor's details (250 chats a month).
  • A booking page for volunteer days, orientations and meet-and-greets with rescue horses, with reminders (100 text and 300 email reminders a month).
  • Missed-call text-back on the ranch number, because whoever answers the phone is usually with a horse (from the shared 1,900 texts a month).
  • A phone receptionist trained on the ranch's FAQ, 500 minutes a month. It takes a message on horse surrender and welfare calls and passes urgent ones straight to Braxton.
  • A business number that calls and texts comes with the plan. Texting registration is filed from inside the app and approved by the carriers in about 1 to 3 business days.

E. Get found and trusted: Echo, Repute, Marquee

  • Create or claim the Google Business Profile and manage it: one name, one set of hours, the donate and volunteer links, photos, one horse update a week, and a 9-point Maps rank grid around Norman.
  • A review request to every visitor and volunteer after a volunteer day or meet-and-greet (300 requests a month), never only the happy ones, because Google bans review gating. A drafted reply to every review for Braxton to approve.
  • Social scheduling to up to 3 accounts (Instagram, Facebook and one more) from one calendar. The class, then the intern, write the two-a-week horse series; Marquee posts it on time and tags the donors and partners each post names.

F. Find sponsors and partners: Radar

  • Five new local prospects a week in up to 3 categories (equestrian programs, feed and tack stores, veterinary clinics), each with business details and a phone number, delivered into the partner pipeline for the class's sponsorship asks.

G. Set-up, reporting and hand-over

  • Station sets up every product above in the first 30 days (Section 06).
  • A monthly report of donations, volunteers, bookings, calls, chats, reviews and list growth, sent automatically each month.
  • A one-hour training for the OU intern in January 2027, and a written guide to the weekly routine: post the series, send the monthly email, check the pipeline.

What runs automatically: the 29 automations

Workstreams B to G run as 29 automations inside the ranch's Station app. Each one starts on its own trigger. Every message is pre-written in the ranch's voice, signed by the ranch, and approved by Braxton in a one-hour review before go-live.

Approval column. Template: approved once, in the one-hour review. Each time: waits for Braxton's approval on every send. Script and Facts: the receptionist and chat answer only from a script and fact sheet Braxton approves. —: an alert or a record, nothing sent to the public.

Built-in protections. A gift is never counted or thanked twice. No one gets more than 6 messages a day. Unsubscribes are honored everywhere. No email leaves until the ranch's own mailbox is connected, and no text until its carrier registration is approved. Donations stay off until the ranch's EIN, legal name, address and its own Stripe account are in place.

Every message any product sends is pre-written in the ranch's voice (hopeful, accountable, personal, as the brief sets it), editable in the app, and approved by Braxton before it goes live. The ranch's name and number are on everything; Station stays backstage.

05Out of scope

Anything not listed in Section 03 is out of scope and needs a change order (Section 09). In particular:

  • The campaign's own work: the press story, meet-and-greet events, equestrian partnerships and in-person asks belong to the class and the ranch. Station supplies the links, booking page and pipeline they run on.
  • Filming and editing the recovery video ad, and buying the ad's media. Running ads is in Marquee Max ($597 a month on its own, or in the Max plan); ad spend is always extra.
  • Social posts and email updates written for the ranch. Core is self-serve, written by the class and the intern; Pro ($2,497 a month at list) drafts 12 posts and 2 emails a month for approval; Max ($3,897 a month at list) writes and sends them.
  • Text-to-give (Tap Pro, in the Pro plan). Core includes one-time and monthly gifts, Sponsor a horse and invoices.
  • Photography and video of the horses. The ranch supplies photos and footage, or approves the class's.
  • Horse care, intake, adoption decisions and the cost figures themselves. Station publishes the ranch's numbers; it does not produce them.
  • Grant writing, fundraising events, printed flyers and signage.
  • Legal, tax or accounting advice, including the wording of donation tax receipts. The ranch confirms its receipt wording with its own adviser.

09Assumptions, dependencies and change control

This SOW rests on facts the class's brief is still asking the ranch for. The ranch confirms each one, or replaces it in writing, before signature.

Assumptions confirmed before signature

  • Today's number of donors and volunteers, the target for each, and the date.
  • The primary audience: donors, volunteers or community members.
  • The proposition: Option A, B or C from the brief.
  • Giving by card, Apple Pay, Google Pay or bank on the ranch's own Stripe (one-time, monthly and Sponsor a horse) as the main way to give, with Venmo, GoFundMe and checks as secondary options.
  • The monthly cost of caring for one horse, itemized, or a commitment to start tracking it per horse.
  • Which horses the site and series can follow by name, and whether they can be filmed where they are now.
  • Access to the Wix site and domain, the Facebook and Instagram accounts, and the Google listing, if one exists. (Braxton Brackin is Founder & CEO; Anna Foote is Barn Manager & Instructor; Chloe Bjornberg is Instructor and website administrator.)
  • When the busy season starts, and any words or images the campaign must not use.

Dependencies

  • Week 1 work starts only when Google and social access are granted and the Stripe account exists.
  • Texting depends on the EIN and carrier approval, about 1 to 3 business days after the registration is filed from the app.
  • Message templates go live only after the ranch's one-hour review.
  • Where Your Gift Goes depends on the ranch's cost sheet; the page launches with the horses and adds the costs when the sheet arrives.
  • The series continuing after December depends on an OU intern being placed by January 2027, which the class arranges.

Change control

  1. Either party asks for a change in writing.
  2. Station replies within 3 business days with the effect on scope, dates and price.
  3. The change takes effect only when both parties approve it in writing.
  4. Moving between Core, Pro and Max follows the same process and takes effect on the next billing date.
